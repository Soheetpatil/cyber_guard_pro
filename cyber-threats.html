Phishing Attacks

Critical Risk

Sophisticated social engineering attacks designed to steal credentials and sensitive information through deceptive communications.

Attack Vectors

  • Email spoofing and domain impersonation
  • Malicious links and attachments
  • Fake login pages and credential harvesting
  • Business email compromise (BEC)

Warning Signs

  • Urgent or threatening language
  • Generic greetings and poor grammar
  • Suspicious sender addresses
  • Unexpected requests for sensitive data

of cyberattacks start with phishing

Ransomware

High Risk

Malicious software that encrypts files and systems, demanding payment for decryption keys while causing significant operational disruption.

Common Variants

  • Crypto-ransomware (file encryption)
  • Locker ransomware (system lockout)
  • Double extortion attacks
  • Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS)

Entry Points

  • Email attachments and links
  • Remote desktop protocol (RDP) attacks
  • Software vulnerabilities
  • Malicious websites and downloads
68%
of organizations hit by ransomware
21 days
average recovery time

Social Engineering

Medium Risk

Psychological manipulation techniques used to trick individuals into divulging confidential information or performing actions that compromise security.

Common Tactics

  • Pretexting and impersonation
  • Baiting with malicious media
  • Tailgating and physical access
  • Quid pro quo exchanges

Target Areas

  • Help desk and IT support
  • Reception and front desk staff
  • New employees and contractors
  • Executive assistants

of attacks rely on social engineering

Advanced Persistent Threats

High Risk

Sophisticated, long-term cyberattacks where attackers gain unauthorized access and remain undetected for extended periods.

Attack Phases

  • Initial reconnaissance and targeting
  • Initial compromise and foothold
  • Lateral movement and escalation
  • Data exfiltration and persistence

Indicators

  • Unusual network traffic patterns
  • Unauthorized access attempts
  • Suspicious file modifications
  • Anomalous user behavior

average time to detect
80 days
average time to contain

AI-Powered Attacks

High Impact

Cybercriminals are increasingly leveraging artificial intelligence and machine learning to create more sophisticated and targeted attacks.

Key Developments:

  • AI-generated deepfake videos for social engineering
  • Machine learning-powered password cracking
  • Automated vulnerability discovery and exploitation
  • Intelligent evasion of traditional security controls
400% increase in AI-assisted attacks
73% of security teams unprepared

Supply Chain Attacks

Critical

Attackers are targeting software supply chains to compromise multiple organizations simultaneously through trusted third-party vendors.

Attack Vectors:

  • Compromised software updates and patches
  • Malicious code injection in open-source libraries
  • Hardware tampering during manufacturing
  • Cloud service provider compromises
742% increase in supply chain attacks
62% lack vendor security oversight

Cloud-Native Threats

High Impact

As organizations migrate to cloud environments, attackers are developing cloud-specific attack techniques and tools.

Emerging Threats:

  • Container escape and privilege escalation
  • Serverless function manipulation
  • Cloud storage misconfigurations
  • Identity and access management bypasses

Mobile & IoT Exploitation

Medium Impact

The proliferation of mobile devices and IoT sensors creates new attack surfaces that are often poorly secured.

Common Vulnerabilities:

  • Weak authentication and encryption
  • Insecure firmware and software updates
  • Default credentials and configurations
  • Insufficient network segmentation
